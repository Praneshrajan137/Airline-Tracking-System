# Traveler research protocol

**Study status:** materials prepared; five moderated sessions are still pending. No participants have been recruited or contacted, and no results have been collected.

## Purpose

Test whether travelers or people following a flight can identify the intended trip when a flight number has multiple dated matches, understand the source and freshness labels, and find an instance-specific summary useful. This study tests the product hypothesis; it must not be used to imply that the product is already validated.

## Method

- Conduct five one-to-one moderated sessions, about 30–40 minutes each.
- Use the local fixture demo only. Do not enter participant flight details, credentials, or provider account data.
- Ask for consent before taking notes. Do not record audio, video, or screens unless separate, explicit consent is given.
- Use participant codes P01–P05. Keep contact details separate from notes and delete them after scheduling and follow-up are complete.
- Do not promise accuracy, live tracking, or service availability. Explain that the demo uses sample records.

## Session script

1. **Opening and consent (3 min):** Explain the academic/portfolio purpose, sample data, note-taking, voluntary participation, and option to stop. Ask whether note-taking is okay.
2. **Current workflow (5 min):** “Tell me about the last time you needed to check a flight for yourself or someone else. What did you do, and what was difficult?” Do not suggest a problem.
3. **Unprompted task (5 min):** Show the start screen and ask: “Find the flight you mean for UAL123 and tell me what you think is happening.” Do not explain the candidate list first.
4. **Selection and identity (5 min):** Ask the participant to choose the intended route/date. Ask what made them choose it and what they would do if the matches did not look right.
5. **Source and freshness (5 min):** Ask what “Demo fixture,” “checked at,” and the position timestamp/age mean to them. Ask whether they would rely on this result and what else they would check.
6. **Summary usefulness (5 min):** Ask what the selected-flight summary adds, whether it changes their next step, and what information is missing.
7. **Comparison and close (5 min):** Ask how this compares with their usual tracker, what they would keep/remove, and whether any label created a wrong impression.

## Tasks and measures

| Measure | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Task completion | Completed without help / completed with help / not completed |
| Time to intended flight | Seconds from task prompt to chosen candidate |
| Wrong-instance selection | Whether a different date or route was chosen and whether the participant noticed |
| Confidence | Participant rating from 1 (not confident) to 5 (very confident), with reason |
| Source comprehension | What the participant believes “Demo fixture” means |
| Freshness comprehension | What “checked at” and position age mean; whether they infer live tracking |
| Summary usefulness | Rating from 1 to 5 plus the decision or action it changes, if any |
| Friction and trust | Quotes or observed hesitation, confusion, or over-trust, without names or itinerary details |

Do not combine five small sessions into statistical or population-level claims. Summarize repeated themes and dissenting observations, and report the sample size beside every finding.
